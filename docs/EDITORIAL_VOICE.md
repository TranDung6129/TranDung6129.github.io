# Editorial Voice Guide — The Epistemic Diary

## Voice
**Research like a scientist. Explain like a great teacher. Tell it like a witty friend.**
Our audience may be eating dinner while watching YouTube or reading on a commute, but deserves intellectual respect. Make complex science, mathematics, technology, finance and society feel inviting.

## Tone spectrum
- Approachable, curious, mildly playful, occasionally dry/satirical.
- Deeply researched and accountable when stating facts.
- Comfortable with uncertainty and reasonable disagreement.
- Never contemptuous toward learners or substitutes jokes for evidence.
- Avoid stiff textbook intros and empty sensationalism.

## Narrative arc for essays and videos
1. An intriguing, defensible claim or paradox.
2. A familiar, concrete situation.
3. The model/mechanism that explains what is happening.
4. The uncomfortable edge case, tradeoff or open question.
5. Honest implications, source trail, and what remains unknown.

## Productive debate vs deception
- Strong hooks are welcome if the body substantiates them.
- Distinguish demonstrated fact, interpretation, hypothesis and editorial opinion.
- Do not invent numeric claims such as "90% of programmers..." without evidence.
- Never deliberately omit conditions needed to interpret a claim correctly.
- Close with a real unresolved question, not a manufactured false dilemma.
- If wrong, publish clear correction/updated date and cite the revision.

## Long-form video approach
- **Meal-time friendly:** jokes from analogies, rhythmic storytelling, warm voice, understandable without staring at equations continuously.
- **Deep films:** original research/storyboard, animation inspired by mathematical visual storytelling, experiments and citations; target quality over schedule volume.
- Companion article is the durable source of references, transcripts, proofs and reproducible code.
- TikTok/Shorts generate curiosity; do not mutilate conclusions to force engagement.
- No cloning creators' voices, signature graphics, scripts or identities.

## Example editorial transformation
Textbook: "Gradient descent is an iterative optimization algorithm."
Our voice: "Imagine being dropped onto a mountain blindfolded. You can only feel which way the ground slopes. Congratulations: you're now a gradient-based optimizer. Whether you reach the *best* valley is another matter."
Then explain the gradient and limits correctly.

## Headlines and calls to action
- Good: "AI can write code. Who gets to maintain it?"
- Good: "99% accuracy, zero patients found: what went wrong?"
- Avoid: "Science has proven X" when evidence supports only one specific setup.
- Membership CTA: support independent explanations and future experiments, not false urgency.
- Affiliate CTA: explain who a product suits and who should skip it; never pretend to have tested equipment we haven't.

## Editorial checklist
Every substantive piece has: claim audit; source list; examples; fact vs opinion distinction; no spurious numbers; appropriate image/code rights; context and limitations; byline; update/corrections policy. The piece should still be useful if the jokes are removed.
